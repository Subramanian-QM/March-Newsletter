Editor's Perspective

The February–March 2026 period has been consequential across every pillar of the quality profession. India is tightening its ESG architecture, the GCC is accelerating from policy ambition to operational delivery, and ISO 9001:2026 signals that quality leadership now carries an explicit cultural and ethical mandate. The central insight: Quality, Excellence, Sustainability, and Governance are no longer parallel tracks — they are converging into a single strategic discipline. This edition is structured to reflect that reality.

Section 01

Quality — Systems, Standards & Infrastructure

01
QCI Reform 🇮🇳 India

QCI Launches Next-Generation Quality Infrastructure for Viksit Bharat 2047

The Quality Council of India has announced sweeping reforms to strengthen India's quality ecosystem. The flagship initiative is the Q Mark – Desh ka Haq — a QR-coded mark of quality for laboratories, hospitals, and MSMEs enabling citizens to verify accreditation credentials instantly, effectively eliminating fake certificates from the system.

A single paperless accreditation portal replaces multiple legacy portals. The new Quality Setu platform provides ticket-based, time-bound grievance resolution. Fast-track accreditation pathways are being introduced for high-growth sectors including drones and cybersecurity. A Quality Passport will enable faster global market access for Indian products and services.

QCI Infrastructure Reforms — Key Actions 2026

  • ZED & Lean certification fees reduced for MSME affordability
  • NABL — 48-hour scope extensions for similar products/methods
  • NABL — self-declaration for authorised signatories, 48-hr approvals
  • NABH MITRA — verified mentors empanelled for smaller town hospitals
  • Single paperless accreditation portal replacing multiple portals
  • Quality Passport for faster global market access for Indian products
Testing & Labs 🇮🇳 India

India's Vision: Become the Global Testing Capital of the World

NABL reforms under QCI's 2026 roadmap target positioning India as the world's preferred testing destination. A model scope for medical testing laboratories will ensure uniformity across accredited scopes. 5,000 laboratory personnel will be trained in 2026 under technical skilling initiatives to strengthen India's national testing infrastructure.

AI-assisted desktop surveillance is being piloted for consistently performing hospitals under NABH, reducing the burden of physical inspections while maintaining rigour. The graded penalty structure introduced under NABH replaces blanket penalties with corrective guidance, marking a shift from punitive to improvement-oriented quality governance.

ISO 9001:2026 🌐 Global

ISO 9001:2026 — Draft Approved, September Publication on Track

The Draft International Standard (DIS) for ISO 9001:2026 has been formally approved by ISO member bodies. Target publication: September 2026, followed by a three-year transition period. This is an evolution, not a revolution — organisations with mature QMS systems face minimal disruption, while those in early stages gain clearer guidance.

"ISO 9001 is a cornerstone for quality assurance across industries. This revision ensures the standard remains relevant in today's increasingly complex and dynamic business environments." — DNV, Global Management Systems

Key changes include mandatory climate change consideration in context analysis (Clause 4), expanded leadership accountability to promote quality culture and ethical behaviour, AI/data analytics incorporated into quality methodology guidance, and clarified risk and opportunity management with broadened awareness requirements.

Excellence — Culture, Leadership & Industry Movements

02
MSME Excellence 🇮🇳 India

IFQM MSME Symposium 2026 at Anna University Catalyses India's Industry-Led Quality Movement

The Indian Foundation for Quality Management (IFQM) held its MSME Symposium on March 20, 2026 at Anna University, Guindy, Chennai — bringing together 250+ CEOs from the MSME sector alongside CPOs, business leaders, and quality ambassadors. Together with Bosch, IFQM convened an exclusive knowledge-sharing session on how quality management shapes the future of Indian manufacturing.

The IFQM movement — a Section 8 not-for-profit — continues to build the "Indian Way" of quality: a systemic, industry-led approach designed to catalyse Indian industries toward global competitiveness through quality, innovation, and excellence.

GCC Excellence 🌍 GCC

GCC Giga Projects Set Global Benchmark for Operational Excellence at Scale

Saudi Arabia's giga projects — NEOM, Red Sea Global, Diriyah, Qiddiya, and ROSHN — collectively valued at over SAR 2 trillion, are redefining operational excellence at national scale. Red Sea Global has transitioned from construction to early operations, with 15 assets live, 100% treated sewage effluent reuse, and 70% operational waste diversion — establishing a new reference point for integrated quality and sustainability management.

The ET Edge GCC Summit 2026 recognised GCCs and leaders who have moved beyond operational excellence to demonstrate leadership in innovation, talent development, governance, and measurable business impact — reflecting the broader pivot of GCCs from support hubs to strategic innovation engines.

Global Excellence Forum 🌐 Global

ASQ Celebrates 80 Years: WCQI 2026 Focuses on AI, Quality 4.0, and Future Leadership

2026 marks the American Society for Quality's 80th anniversary. Its flagship World Conference on Quality and Improvement (WCQI) — themed "When Purpose and Quality Meet, Magic Happens" — convenes May 17–20 in Orlando, Florida. The conference spotlights Quality 4.0 and Quality 5.0 methodologies, AI-enhanced quality tools, and future-ready leadership development.

💡 Practitioner Tip of the Month

Preparing Your QMS for ISO 9001:2026 — Start Now, Not Later

Don't wait for the final publication in September 2026. Begin by reviewing your Clause 4 context analysis — add climate-related risks and opportunities as an explicit category. Next, engage your top management on what "promoting quality culture" means in your specific organisational context and how it will be demonstrated — not just stated in a policy. Finally, review your risk register through the lens of AI adoption: if your organisation is introducing AI-based tools, your QMS should already be capturing the quality and reliability considerations. The organisations that use the transition period proactively will be ahead by 2029.

ESG & Sustainability — Disclosures, Climate & Green Finance

03
SEBI BRSR 🇮🇳 India

India's BRSR 2026: The Year ESG Data Earns the Same Weight as Financial Statements

India's market regulator SEBI has entered the pivotal enforcement phase of its BRSR framework. Mandatory third-party verification begins with the Top 500 listed companies in FY2025–26, expanding to the Top 1,000 in FY2026–27. Value chain ESG metrics are now voluntarily reportable, with mandatory assurance following thereafter.

The BRSR Core framework is extending into the supply chains of key industries including technology and consumer goods. SEBI's amendments also introduce the Green Credit Programme as a leadership indicator under Principle 6, where companies and their top ten value chain partners can earn credits for afforestation and river catchment restoration.

GCC ESG 🌍 GCC

GCC's $58 Billion ESG Investment Surge: From Ambition to Measurable Action

Environmental investment across the GCC reached a record $58 billion between November 2024 and March 2025. KPMG projects over $60 billion in renewable energy investment by end-2026. ESG regulation across all six member states has moved beyond policy frameworks into disclosure mandates with supervisory oversight.

GCC ESG Regulatory Snapshot — All Six States

  • UAE: UAE Climate Law — full GHG compliance deadline 30 May 2026
  • Saudi Arabia: Tadawul ESG disclosure guidelines for all listed companies
  • Qatar: QSE sustainability platform — mandatory ESG reporting for listed companies
  • Oman: Mandatory ESG reporting for listed companies from 2025 onward
  • Bahrain: CBB-enforced ESG reporting covering Scope 1, 2 & 3 since 2024
  • Kuwait: Kuwait 2035 Vision embeds ESG into economic diversification
Global ESG Divergence 🌐 Global

India Holds the Line as US Retreats and EU Delays on ESG Reporting

Three major regulatory jurisdictions have diverged sharply. The US SEC announced it will no longer defend its climate disclosure rule in court. The EU voted to delay mandatory ESG reporting for smaller companies until 2028. India, by contrast, has maintained regulatory direction under SEBI while offering phased flexibility — making India's structured approach a potential reference point for developing economies seeking to unlock ESG finance.

Section 04

Regulations & Standards — Policy, Compliance & Governance

04
Procurement Compliance 🌍 GCC

ISO Certification Now a Commercial Prerequisite Across GCC Government Procurement

In Saudi Arabia's procurement-driven economy, ISO certification has become non-negotiable. Government and semi-government tenders on the Etimad Platform score certified suppliers higher in technical evaluations — missing certification means leaving contracts on the table. In the UAE, free zone authorities in DMCC and DIFC now require certified suppliers to satisfy both revised ISO 9001 criteria and additional free zone authority requirements during tender evaluations.

Standards Convergence 🌐 Global

A Historic Alignment: ISO 9001, Aerospace IA9100, IATF 16949, and CMMC Converge

The 2026–2028 period marks an unprecedented convergence of quality and compliance standards. In aerospace and defence, AS9100 is evolving into IA9100 — aligning with ISO revisions and incorporating tighter supply-chain and digital assurance practices. IATF 16949 (automotive) is simultaneously being modernised. U.S. defence suppliers are preparing for CMMC cybersecurity certification. For quality professionals in India and the GCC supplying into global aerospace, automotive, and defence chains — this period is a readiness test and a strategic opportunity to differentiate through early compliance.

Climate Regulation 🌍 UAE

UAE Climate Law Enters Full Force: GHG Compliance Deadline — 30 May 2026

The UAE Climate Law requires all UAE entities to measure, report, and progressively reduce greenhouse gas emissions. Full compliance is required by 30 May 2026. Quality and operations teams in UAE-registered organisations who have not yet completed their Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG inventory should treat this as an immediate priority. The UAE Central Bank's parallel Climate-related Financial Risk Management Regulation extends these expectations to supervised financial institutions.

Section 05 · GCC Spotlight

GCC — Quality, ESG & Excellence in the Gulf

05
UAE

UAE: Where ISO 9001, Operation 300bn, and Net Zero 2050 Converge Into One Mandate

For businesses operating in ICAD, D3, or any of Dubai's free zones, a Quality Management System is no longer a differentiator — it is a minimum entry requirement. The UAE's Operation 300bn industrial strategy and We the UAE 2031 vision are reshaping what regulators and free zone authorities expect from certified organisations, creating a layered compliance environment that rewards proactive quality investment.

The UAE's Net Zero 2050 ambition means quality and sustainability have formally merged in the regulatory calendar. Organisations integrating their QMS and environmental management systems are better positioned to respond to both ISO 9001:2026 and the UAE Climate Law simultaneously — reducing duplication of effort and demonstrating integrated governance maturity.

Saudi Arabia

Saudi Giga Projects: ESG Benchmarking Reveals a Clear Maturity Stratification

A February 2026 ESG maturity assessment of Saudi Arabia's five giga projects reveals distinct performance tiers. Red Sea Global and Diriyah Company rank in the Advanced range; NEOM and Qiddiya in the Developing range; and ROSHN as Emerging — based on a 30-point scorecard drawing on publicly disclosed data.

Red Sea Global leads with 100% TSE water reuse and 70% operational waste diversion. For quality and ESG professionals in the region, these benchmarks provide a visible performance ladder — and a signal of where procurement and investor expectations are heading for suppliers into the giga project ecosystem.

Section 06 · Special Focus

GCC Uncertainty — Risks & Opportunities for Quality Professionals

06
Geopolitical Risk 🌍 GCC Region

Strait of Hormuz Tensions and Oil Price Volatility: What Quality Leaders Need to Know

The GCC entered 2026 in a position of notable economic strength — high hydrocarbon revenues, expanding sovereign wealth portfolios, and sustained diversification programmes. But the escalation of regional conflict involving Israel, Iran, and their allies has exposed the limits of that resilience. Heightened security risks and potential disruptions to tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz — through which roughly 20 million barrels per day of crude and one-fifth of global LNG transits — have injected significant uncertainty into regional supply chains, shipping costs, and investor confidence.

"For GCC economies, geopolitical shocks are transmitted not only through oil prices, but also through trade routes, shipping costs, food and industrial input chains, and shifts in investor sentiment." — OMFIF, March 2026

Oil price forecasts for 2026 range widely. J.P. Morgan Research sees Brent crude averaging around $60/bbl, while ING has revised its forecast upward to $62/bbl citing US–Iran uncertainty. In a tail-risk scenario involving significant Hormuz disruption, Brent could spike well above $100/bbl — a scenario quality and procurement professionals must scenario-plan for.

Trade Disruption 🌍 GCC 🇮🇳 India Impact

US Tariff Reshuffling, Sanctions, and the Fragmentation of GCC Trade Routes

Global trade policy is compounding the geopolitical pressure. More than 3,000 new trade and industrial policy measures were introduced globally in 2025 — over three times the annual level a decade ago. For the GCC, this has accelerated the need to broaden trade relationships beyond traditional partners.

India is directly in the frame: with thinner reserves and heavy reliance on Middle Eastern crude, India is more vulnerable to a prolonged disruption — with higher energy prices feeding inflation, weakening the rupee, and threatening growth. For quality professionals managing India–GCC supply chains, this creates immediate cost and lead-time volatility that must be factored into risk registers and supplier qualification frameworks.

GCC Uncertainty — Impact Radar for Quality & Supply Chain Professionals

  • Shipping & logistics: Rerouting around Hormuz increases lead times and freight costs — update supplier lead time buffers in your QMS
  • Input costs: Petrochemical feedstock and fertiliser price spikes — review supplier financial health in critical categories
  • ISO certification timelines: Assessment travel and auditor availability may be impacted in conflict-proximate zones
  • ESG reporting: Scope 3 emissions may shift significantly as supply routes are redirected — flag in your BRSR/GHG inventory
  • Procurement scoring: UAE and Saudi tender processes continue — certified suppliers retain competitive advantage even in uncertainty
  • Investor confidence: ESG-mature organisations in the GCC show more resilience to geopolitical sentiment shocks
Opportunity Lens 🌍 GCC 🌐 Global

The Silver Lining: Why Uncertainty Accelerates the GCC's Quality & Diversification Agenda

History consistently shows that geopolitical and economic pressure accelerates structural reforms — and the GCC is no exception. Trade diversification, supply-chain security, AI deployment, workforce transitions, and fiscal resilience will shape the GCC's economic trajectory in 2026, as the region deepens global integration and builds foundations of long-term, sustainable growth.

For quality professionals, this creates a clear opportunity window. Organisations that invest now in ISO certification, integrated QMS-ESG frameworks, and supply chain resilience are better positioned to win in GCC markets as governments tighten procurement standards. Nearly three in four global business leaders now prioritise resilience investments, viewing resilience as a driver of growth rather than a cost. That mindset shift is arriving in the GCC — and quality systems are its operational foundation.

The GCC's sovereign wealth buffers, continued giga-project investment momentum, and commitment to Vision 2030/We the UAE 2031 diversification mean the fundamental demand for quality, certification, and ESG-aligned suppliers remains structurally intact — even as the short-term environment is turbulent.
